> **Sources:** messybeast.com colour & pattern guide (Sarah Hartwell), user screenshots — Silver and Golden Ticked Tabbies; Chinchilla (Shell), Tipped (Shorthair Tipped), Shaded and Smoke Colour Group (with its Black-on-Silver, Blue-on-Silver and Red/Cream synonym boxes); Golden Colour Group; Breed Specific Tipped.

## At a glance

- **The source describes a "tipping ladder":** silver tabby, then shell/chinchilla (lightest tipping), shaded, and smoke (heaviest). The golden series runs parallel on a gold rather than white undercoat. "Chinchilla" and "shell" are longhair terms; "tipped" is the shorthair term.
- **The calculator has the genes but not the ladder.** `I` (silver) turns tabby into Silver Tabby, solid into Smoke and red into Cameo. `Wb` plus agouti gives one "Shaded" step (silver) or "Golden Shaded" (no silver). Shell, chinchilla and tipped all come out as "Silver Shaded".
- **Most rows here are naming gaps, not missing genes:** Cameo synonyms (Shell Cameo, Smoke Cameo and so on), Pewter, Masked, "Usual/Ruddy/Sorrel" for silver ticked Abyssinians, and "Tipped" vs "Chinchilla". A synonym/dialect layer would cover them.
- **Golden smoke cannot exist** (golden + non-agouti = solid). The engine already agrees: Wb is carried but "needs agouti to show".
- **Counts:** 8 silver/golden ticked rows, 12 chinchilla-group rows, 6 black-on-silver, 6 blue-on-silver, 13 red/cream/blue-cream formula entries, 9 golden-group examples, 1 Chausie row and 5 glossary terms.
- **Outside the model:** the Chausie "Silver Tipped" (wild-derived), Bimetallic (unofficial), and anything depending on eye colour (Pewter).

## Chinchilla (Shell), Tipped (Shorthair Tipped), Shaded, and Smoke Colour Group

Chinchilla (shell) is the lightest tipping: the hair tip is coloured and the shaft is silver, giving a sparkling appearance. Shaded is the next degree, with colour extending further along the shaft and darkest on the back, forming a mantle of shading. Smoke is the heaviest tipping: the undercoat is reduced to a small band near the root, so the cat looks solid with a pale ruff/frill until the coat is parted or the cat moves. In the golden series the undercoat is gold rather than white. "Chinchilla" and "shell" are mostly used for longhairs (Persians); in shorthairs this is called tipped.

## Modelling notes

- **Modelled:** `I` (silver: tabby → Silver Tabby, solid → Smoke, red → Cameo), `Wb` with agouti (Shaded when silver, Golden when not), and ticked silver via Ta + I. The torties on silver (shell, shaded, smoke, patched) all come from combining O/o with I, Wb and a/a.
- **One shaded step only:** the engine tests only "has a Wb allele", so Wb/wb and Wb/Wb read the same, and nothing separates shell/chinchilla from shaded. My inference (the source does not say): degree of tipping is usually treated as polygenic modifiers or `Wb` dose. If so, a "tipping depth" note or slider, not more alleles, would be the right model. The source's own line "Tipping ranges from shell (chinchilla) to shaded" fits a continuum.
- **"Chinchilla/shell" vs "tipped" is a coat-length naming dialect** (longhair vs shorthair). Inference: the existing `L` locus could choose the word with no new genetics.
- **Cameo family and historic synonyms are naming-only:** the calculator says "Cameo …", "Shaded Cameo", "Red Smoke" and "Dilute Cameo …". Not produced: Shell Cameo, Smoke Cameo, Shaded Cream Cameo, Cream Cameo Tabby, Smoke Cream Cameo, "Shell Dilute Tortoiseshell" and similar.
- **Silver ticked works only when Wb is absent.** With Ta + I + Wb/- the engine names it "Silver Shaded" and drops the ticked word; golden ticked likewise reads "Golden Shaded". Whether real silver Abyssinians are wb/wb is my assumption.
- **Golden is "Wb without I":** golden tabby, golden chinchilla and golden shaded are one step. Golden smoke is correctly impossible.
- **Smoke is a different mechanism in the engine (a/a + I)** from the agouti ladder (Wb + I). The source presents smoke as the heaviest tipping step. This is my reading of how the two fit together; the source does not discuss it.
- **Descriptive or selected, not genotypes:** Masked Silver (extent of dark face/paws), Pewter (orange eyes), Bimetallic (unofficial). Show these as notes. Pewter's defining feature is eye colour, which the calculator does not model.
- **Silver's green eyes are breeder choice, not linkage:** pedigree silvers are bred for green eyes, but this is selection, not genetic linkage to the silver gene. See [06-eye-colour-and-shape.md](06-eye-colour-and-shape.md) for how the portrait picks eye colour. (This point comes from the brief, not the source tables, apart from the Shaded Silver "blue/green eyes" and Pewter "orange-eyed" cells.)
- **Chausie Silver Tipped is wild-derived** (jungle-cat ancestry) and outside the Mendelian model; the grizzle and reversed-ticking entries suggest a "light tip on a dark hair" feature that the engine has no concept of.
- **Torbie wording stops at shaded:** "Silver Patched Tabby" is modelled ("Silver Torbie — … pattern") but "Golden Patched Tabby" becomes "Golden Shaded Tortoiseshell", because the engine only says Torbie when not shaded. See also [02](02-tabby-patterns.md).

## Source quirks & open questions

- **"Silver Tabby" appears twice** (chinchilla group; black on silver) with different wording; both are kept. The first uses the red example "Red-Silver (Cameo) Tabby".
- **Duplicate/overlapping tortie formula entries:** "Red Silver Tortoiseshell/Tortie Tabby" and "Cream Silver Tortoiseshell/Tortie Tabby" have no synonym, and the latter is probably the same colour as "Blue Silver Tortoiseshell/Tortie Tabby" (a dilute silver tortie). The source does not say which is meant.
- **"Silver Tipped"** in the Chausie box and the **Chinchilla** entry's "(called Burmilla in Asian group of cats)" both use a breed name where a colour name would be expected.
- **Chinchilla group header vs Black-on-Silver box:** the header says "chinchilla/shell" are longhair terms and "tipped" is the shorthair term, and the box confirms "aka Silver/Silver Chinchilla/Tipped". The Blue-on-Silver list then uses "Chinchilla" for both.
- **Missing "ticked" in a description:** "Chocolate Silver Ticked Tortoiseshell" is described as "Chocolate tortoiseshell on silver background", so "ticked" is probably missing. Also, "Silver (Silver Usual/Silver Ruddy)" has a trailing comma in the source.
- **"Golden" has two meanings in the source:** a neighbouring ticked table says "Golden" there "is not the same as 'Gold' mentioned further up this page". Treat Ocicat "Golden/Gold" names in [02](02-tabby-patterns.md) as colour names rather than the Wb golden series unless stated.
- **Pewter's history:** "orange-eyed … this was the original eye colour of the breed, but fell out of favour in the 1890s" is a historical claim worth verifying; no source is given.
- **"The Alaskan Snow Cat had the Silver Abyssinian coat pattern"** (past tense) is stated without detail; the breed's current status is unclear.
- **Golden ticked** (Golden Ticked Tabby, Chocolate Golden Ticked Tabby) is accepted in the source, but there is no discussion of how ticking and the golden (wide-band) effect interact; the genotypes above are guesses.
- **Smoke as "heaviest tipping"** (header) vs smoke as non-agouti + silver (calculator) is not reconciled by the source.
- **Cross-reference needed:** the source's ticked/agouti colour table (not covered here) defines Sorrel, Usual and Ruddy, which the silver ticked rows rely on.
