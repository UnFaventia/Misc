## At a glance

- These colours are found in other species. Some have been seen in cats but never standardised or developed. Some might arrive by outcrossing to wild cats, as rosetting and marbling did in the Bengal.
- **Two categories matter for the calculator:**
  1. **Things that already happen** but aren't modelled: chimeras and mosaics beyond XY/XY, satin, pink-eyed dilution, roan.
  2. **Hypotheticals**, which could live in a clearly labelled "sandbox" if you ever want them.

| **Banded (Belted)** | Solid colour with a solid band of white around the middle of the body (as in Dutch rabbits). Some Spanish street cats already show this pattern, but it hasn't been developed | Unknown white-spotting variant | ❌ |
| **Sheeted** | Solid colour with a wide band of colour (shoulder to haunches), as in some breeds of cattle | Unknown | ❌ |

Overall colour distribution like a Bicolour Ragdoll (saddle, mask, white blaze). Blue eyes. Possibly chimeras (two fused embryos) | Chimera/mosaic, e.g. XX/XX with lines differing at D | ❌ |
| **Other Tricolours** | Look like torties (red with small black spots or patches) but are genetically red/red tabby with localised skin mutations, or chimaeras. More and more often seen, giving grey-black-and-white, red-blue-and-white and cream-black-and-white cats. A **Black-Yellow-White** mutant has occurred that resembled a Jack Russell terrier, with a pointed face, long ears and bowed back legs. These cats breed as bicolours, depending on which embryo's cells form the ovaries or testes; others are sterile due to XXY makeup. These quirks account for tortie tomcats | Somatic mosaic · chimera · XXY | 🟡 XXY ✅ and XY/XY ✅; somatic mosaics and XX/XX or XX/XY chimeras ❌ |
| **New colourpoints** | Dark points on a solid coloured body (as in dun horses) or white/pale points on a dark body (some pig breeds) | Unknown | ❌ |
| **New tabbies** | Horizontal stripes; reversed tabby patterns (light markings on a darker base); true spotting; clouding & marbling. Some are seen in wild cat species | Unknown / wild-derived | ❌ (the calculator's `Sp` spots are broken mackerel, not "true" spotting) |
| **Black and Tan** | Black upper body and outside legs, tan lower body and inside legs, as in Doberman dogs, where it's an agouti mutation. A sharp dividing line between black and tan (a "waterline") | Hypothetical ASIP allele (like dog `aᵗ`) | ❌ |
| **Blue and Tan** | Dilute of black-and-tan: dove-grey upper (agouti mutation). Other colours and tan might then be possible | Hypothetical ASIP allele + `d/d` | ❌ |
| **Zebra, Dalmatian, Appaloosa** | Striped and spotted like the dogs and horses of those names | Unknown | ❌ |
| **Pink/Red Eyed Dilutes** | Reappeared in **Donskoy** cats! Pale tan with yellow eyes whose pupils reflect pink | Recessive pink-eyed dilution (see [06](06-eye-colour-and-shape.md#pink-eyed-dilution)) | ❌ |
| **Satin** | Recently appeared in the **Tennessee Rex** and **American Satin**. Not a colour but a fur type that changes how colour looks, adding a sheen because the hair reflects light differently (as in mice). A form of "**glitter**" is seen in some Bengals | Unknown | ❌ |
| **Green** | Not found in any mammal. The famous Danish green kitten was a temporary colour caused by copper contamination | Not genetic | 🚫 |

| Kind | Source mentions | Calculator now | Possible extension |

## Modelling notes

*Design suggestions, not source content.*

- **Cheapest real win:** an **XX/XX chimera**. The XY/XY machinery (two genotypes plus a germline pick) generalises directly, and it explains quadricolours.
- **A sandbox for hypotheticals.** If you want them, keep them behind a "hypothetical alleles" toggle so they can never leak into "real" odds:
  - black-and-tan as an ASIP allele
  - pink-eyed dilution
  - satin
  - banded/belted
- **Satin and glitter** are coat-surface effects. They'd be a portrait shader plus a coat-line word, not a colour change.

## Source quirks & open questions

- **"Sheeted":** described as "solid colour with a wide band **of colour**". In cattle, a sheeted pattern is a wide *white* band, so the wording probably means a wide band of **white** from shoulder to haunches, i.e. a broader version of "banded".
- **The Black-Yellow-White "Jack Russell" mutant:** was it a chimera, or a separate mutation with skeletal effects (bowed back legs)? The table doesn't say.
- **Donskoy pink-eyed dilutes** vs Todd's 1961 cat: is it the same mutation? Unknown.
