## At a glance

- Every tortie row (plain, dilute, amber, chocolate, lilac, ticked, tabby-tortie, silver) is a combination of loci the calculator already has: O/o with A, B, D, E, I and Ta. Most differences are naming only.
- "Tortie and white", "Calico", "Bicolour" and the Van colours all depend on the amount of white. The calculator splits this into Ws/w (mild spotting, tuxedo, bicolor) and Ws/Ws (harlequin, van). The source diagram has 10 grades, so a 10-to-7 mapping is proposed below (my inference).
- Naming gaps: Burmese-dialect torties (Brown, Chocolate, Cinnamon), "Auburn", "Torbico", "Parti-Colour", "Magpie", the Seychellois septieme/huitieme/neuvieme names, Lavender/Chestnut synonyms.
- Not modelled: Ragdoll/Snowshoe mitted genetics (the calculator's "gloves" is only the Birman wg allele), laces, roan/tweed/salt-and-pepper, merle, a possible separate locket/belly-spot gene (the diagram's grades 2 and 3), and eye colour.
- Amount of white is polygenic in the calculator's own blurb. The diagram's grades are a continuous scale, so a range or note suits them better than a single Mendelian result.

## Ticked Tortoiseshells

Because agouti is a type of tabby, ticked (agouti) colours can combine with tortoiseshell markings to give ticked tortoiseshells; some are hard to tell apart because ticking obscures the colours. Ticked colours and ticked tortie colours can be patched with white to give ticked bicolours, but this is presently only seen in non-pedigree cats.

## Broken Colours – Tortoiseshells, Bicolours & Tricolours

Tortoiseshell is the mixing of two or more distinct colours, one of which is red or cream. The black/orange tortie is the familiar one. In pedigree cats, well-defined patches of each colour are preferred. Cats with tabby markings on a tortie background are known as tabby-torties, patched tabbies or torbies. Where the hairs are mixed together the cat is called brindled. Almost all tortie-and-white cats are female; males do occur sometimes but they are either infertile or have a genetic aberration, and do not breed true.

## White Spotting Grades

The messybeast diagram places ten grades of white spotting along an arrow of increasing white: grade 1 non-white spotted, grades 2–4 low grade (<40% white), grade 5 medium grade (40%–60%, "Bicolour") and grades 6–10 high grade (>60% white). Colloquial terms sit in a row beneath the grades and geneticist terms in a row beneath that. Terms are matched to grades by horizontal position in the diagram.

| Grade | Band (% white) | Description | Colloquial term(s) | Geneticist term |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Non-white spotted | Has not inherited main white spotting gene. | — | — |
| 2 ★ | Low grade (<40%) | Small white spots e.g. lockets, white toes, belly spot. | Mitted ★ (may have other small white spots). It is drawn between grades 2 and 3, nearer 3. | — |
| 3 ★ | Low grade (<40%) | White chest and some white spots e.g. toes, belly. | Mitted ★ (see grade 2) | — |
| 4 | Low grade (<40%) | White chest, belly, feet e.g. "tuxedo pattern". | Tuxedo: white mitts, chest, belly & nose; may have white tail tip. | — |
| 5 | Medium grade (40%–60%) | White chest, legs, belly and facial markings. | "Bicolour"; Mask & mantle; Bicolour with freckled legs (the last two are labels only) | — |
| 6 | High grade (>60%) | Mostly white, with colour markings on head, back and tail. | Cap & saddle | — |
| 7 | High grade (>60%) | Mostly white, with small colour markings on body and tail. | Magpie (shares the caption "Pied cats with random spotting." with Harlequin) | Seychellois septieme: patches on head, tail, legs and body. |
| 8 | High grade (>60%) | White, with colour restricted to head, back and tail. | Harlequin (same shared caption as Magpie) | Seychellois huitieme: patches on head, tail and legs only. |
| 9 | High grade (>60%) | Small patches of colour on top of head and on tail. | Van Pattern: splashes between ears. Coloured tail. | Seychellois neuvieme: patches on head and tail only. |
| 10 | High grade (>60%) | Fully white. May have smudge of colour on forehead as a newborn. | — | — |

### Diagram text boxes

**Blue-star footnote** (stars sit on grades 2 and 3 and on Mitted):

> Grades 2 & 3 may have a different gene for white locket and/or belly spots. They will NOT produce higher degrees of white when bred together.

**Red box:**

> Any colour cat can have white spotting, but only solid-and-white, tabby-and-white and tortie-and-white (calico) are recognised in pedigree cats. Markings are typically near-symmetrical.
>
> Apart from Grade 1 (no spotting) and Grade 10 (solid white), any grade of spotting can have small white markings on the toes, belly, chin, chest and nose.

**Blue EYE COLOURS box** (with swatches of blue, amber, green, yellow and deep orange eyes, including odd-eyed pairs):

> White, or mostly white, cats often have blue eyes, amber eyes or one of each colour. Non pedigree white, or mostly white, cats, may have green or yellow eyes.
>
> Non-pedigree colour & mostly-colour cats can have eyes any colour, but rarely blue eyes. In pedigree cats, breed standards define which eye colours are allowed.

## Van, Van Bi-Colour, Harlequin, Seychelles Group

Van pattern is also called Grade 8 – Grade 9 piebald. It is the most extreme of the "Seychellois" patterns, with patches on the head at the base of each ear. The tail is the same colour as the patches, often with darker rings because red and cream are not true solid colours. Van Bi-Colours and Harlequins have additional markings on the legs and one or two small patches on the body. Beware: in Turkey the term "Turkish Van" does not refer to a patterned cat. The Seychellois is a Van-pattern cat of oriental type.

## Modelling notes

- **Tortie rows are naming-only gaps.** O/o in XX, XXY or XY/XY plus A, B, D, E, I and Ta already generate every tortie, torbie, calico and caliby name here. Missing wordings are synonyms: Brown/Chocolate/Cinnamon as Burmese-dialect names (the engine says "Seal Tortie Sepia" etc.), Chestnut, Lavender, Auburn, Torbico, Parti-Colour, Calimanco, Magpie, Flame. A small alias table would close them (my inference). No new locus is needed.
- **Calico threshold.** The engine says "Calico" or "Caliby" only at tuxedo (index 3) and above. The sources say calico for any tortie-and-white and describe parti-colour as roughly one-third colour or less. The threshold is a calculator choice; a decision is needed on whether to keep it.
- **Amount of white is a range, not a Mendelian result.** The calculator's own blurb calls it polygenic, and the diagram is a continuous 10-grade scale. The existing range label ("mild spotting to bicolor", "harlequin to van") fits best. Option (my inference): show the matching grade range and colloquial terms (Tuxedo, Mask & mantle, Cap & saddle, Magpie, Harlequin, Van) as descriptive labels rather than separate genotypes.
- **Possible separate locket/belly-spot gene (diagram footnote).** Grades 2–3 (and the starred Mitted) "may have a different gene" and "will NOT produce higher degrees of white when bred together". If the calculator predicts offspring, treating low-grade white as Ws/w would wrongly predict higher white from two such cats. This needs a new locus or a warning note. Not modelled.
- **Gloves vs mitted.** wg (Birman) is the only gloves/mitts mechanism in the engine. The Ragdoll and Snowshoe mitted patterns, High Mitted, Mid-High White and High White Ragdolls, and laces have no characterised genetics in these sources, so they are not modelled. Treat them as descriptive naming until a gene is identified.
- **Van, harlequin and Seychellois** are naming bands on Ws/Ws (harlequin and van steps). No new locus is needed for the three Seychellois names, only an alias table keyed on step plus a "head, tail, legs, body" description.
- **Solid white (grade 10)** has no Ws step. All-white is currently only Wd (dominant white, with deafness warnings; see [11](11-health-and-welfare.md) and [07](07-dominant-blue-eye.md)).
- **Male torties.** The calculator allows XXY toms and XY/XY chimeras, matching the source's "infertile or genetic aberration". The calculator says chimeras are often fertile, which is not contradicted by "do not breed true". See [10](10-rare-and-future.md) for the source's Quadricolour and Other Tricolours notes.
- **Cross-file overlaps.** Silver, golden and smoke tortie rows overlap [03](03-silver-golden-shading.md) (tipped versus shaded is not distinguished). Tortie points (Seal Tabby Tortoiseshell Point etc.) are in [05](05-colourpoint-mink-sepia-mocha.md).
- **Eye colour** (the diagram's box) is not modelled; see [06](06-eye-colour-and-shape.md) and [07](07-dominant-blue-eye.md). White and mostly-white cats are the cases where the source says blue, amber and odd eyes are common.
- **Notation.** In this file, Ws/- means Ws with any non-Wd partner (w, wg or Ws). Wd/Ws is a white cat. B is left out of the amber genotypes because the engine lets the E locus override it.

## Source quirks & open questions

- **Screenshot overlap.** The Broken Colours table appears in two screenshots with identical rows (14 rows, counted once). The Van colours and Mitted tables also repeat across two screenshots (6 and 8 rows, counted once). Other tables on those screenshots (Silver and Golden Ticked Tabbies, Tabby Colours, Breed-Specific Tabby/Spotted Terms, Chinchilla group, tortie points, Mink, Equivalent Names, Future Colour Possibilities) belong to other files.
- **Seychellois descriptions differ from the diagram's grade 7/8 descriptions.** The diagram says grade 7 has "small colour markings on body and tail" and grade 8 has "colour restricted to head, back and tail". The Seychellois text says septieme has patches on head, tail, legs and body, and huitieme has head, tail and legs only. So grade 7 does not mention the head or legs, and grade 8 includes the back but omits legs, while the Seychellois entries include legs and leave out the back. The Van header says Van is "Grade 8 – Grade 9", but the diagram places Van Pattern at 9 and huitieme at 8.
- **Harlequin/Magpie placement is uncertain.** The diagram puts Magpie at grade 7 and Harlequin at grade 8 under one shared caption ("Pied cats with random spotting"). The glossary defines Harlequin as Van markings plus small body and leg patches, and Bi-Colour as "(Magpie)". The Van header says Van Bi-Colours and Harlequins have extra leg markings and one or two body patches, which reads more like septieme (grade 7) than grade 8.
- **"Bicolour" means three things.** In Broken Colours it is any solid colour plus white; in the diagram it is the single grade 5 (40%–60%); in the glossary "Bi-Colour (Magpie)" is any colour and white. The calculator's "bicolor" is a single step (index 4).
- **Grade 6 versus grade 8 wording overlaps.** Both say colour on "head, back and tail" ("Mostly white, with colour markings on…" versus "White, with colour restricted to…").
- **Mitted has several definitions.** The diagram places Mitted at grades 2–3 with a star ("may have other small white spots"). The plain glossary says white bootees on all four feet (back to the hocks). The show definition says paws, back legs, belly, chest and chin, about one quarter white. "Seal Point Mitted" is described as "Birman pattern … white face blaze, belly, boots & mitts", which is more than the Birman gloves the engine models with wg.
- **The star on Mitted is not explained.** The footnote text names only grades 2 and 3, but the star also sits on Mitted. I read it as applying to Mitted too.
- **"Chocolate Tortoiseshell: Warm milk chocolate, red, and cream."** Cream needs d/d, which would make the chocolate patches lilac. This is probably loose wording for pale patches.
- **"Cinnamon Tortoiseshell: Milk-chocolate brown and cream (Burmese)."** Reads like chocolate, and the "(Burmese)" tag is unexplained. Verify whether it is a Burmese-registry name and whether cinnamon is meant.
- **"Brown Tortoiseshell" versus "Brown (Black, Ebony) Tabby".** "Brown" is the Burmese word for the Seal/sepia colour here but means plain black in the Tabby Colours table. Check whether "brown" implies cb/cb in the Broken Colours row.
- **Ticked bicolours "only in non-pedigree cats"** is a claim about registries, not genetics. The calculator can combine Ta with Ws anyway.
- **Blank cell.** "Chocolate Ticked Tortoiseshell" has a blank description.
- **Garbled source text.** The Septieme row reads "has leg & with body splashes"; I read it as "has leg & body splashes".
- **"Piawaian Kucing Malaysia"** reads like a standards-body name rather than a breed; check before using it as a breed name.
- **Roan/merle and Russian Topaz.** The Russian Topaz black-roan remark comes from the brief, not from these screenshots. Cross-check in [07](07-dominant-blue-eye.md).
