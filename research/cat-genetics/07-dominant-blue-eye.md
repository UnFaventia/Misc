# Dominant Blue Eye (DBE) & the blue-eyed breeds

> **Sources:** Abitbol M, Dufaure de Citres C, Rudd Garces G, Lühken G, Lyons LA, Gache V. *Different Founding Effects Underlie Dominant Blue Eyes (DBE) in the Domestic Cat.* Animals (Basel) 2024;14(13):1845. doi:10.3390/ani14131845, PMID 38997957, PMCID PMC11240321 · messybeast.com (Sarah Hartwell): blue-eyed breeds pages, Altai history, *Bicolours and Eye Colours* (2017) · user diagrams: *Russian Topaz (Altai) eye colours*, *Russian Topaz — white markings linked to blue eye trait*.
> **Calculator status:** ❌ not modelled. There's no PAX3 locus, and eye colour is a portrait heuristic. Of everything in these notes, this is the best-characterised gene the calculator doesn't have yet.
> **Related:** [eye colour & shape](06-eye-colour-and-shape.md) · [white spotting](04-tortie-white-van.md) · [health index](11-health-and-welfare.md) · [coverage map](00-coverage-map.md)

## At a glance

- **"DBE" is a phenotype, not one variant.** One or two blue eyes (or particoloured eyes) plus minimal white spotting. At least **three independent PAX3 alleles** produce it, and four of the 14 lineages genotyped carry none of the three. So there are more DBE genes still to find.
- All known DBE alleles are **autosomal dominant, with incomplete penetrance** (latents carry DBE without blue eyes) **and variable expressivity**.
- **Never cross the alleles.** CEL/ALT compound heterozygotes were white and deaf. The only CEL/CEL kitten was white and died at birth. RE/RE has never been seen and is presumed lethal.
- **Ojos Azules is a separate, earlier mutation and is now extinct.** Its homozygotes had cranial deformities. The modern DBE breeds are unrelated to it.
- **For the calculator:** a new locus `DBE` (PAX3) with four alleles. Eye colour becomes a probabilistic output, the white descriptor needs a "minimal DBE markings" layer, and the litter mode already has the pairing-warning machinery these cases need.

How the ALT allele was found: whole-genome sequencing of one DBE cat, then a panel of **117 DBE cats**. The variant was fully associated with DBE in two British lineages, in Altai cats, and in some other DBE lineages. Then **14 lineages** were genotyped for all three PAX3 variants, and **four** carried none of them. That means the trait is genetically heterogeneous.

> **DNA overturned the folk history.** Breeders had assumed Roxi was an Altai cat and Seymour a separate DBE founder. The genotypes say the reverse: Roxi passed on DBE^CEL, so he was *probably not* Altai, while Seymour passed on DBE^ALT, so he *probably was*. The Topaz diagram's caption "The 'Topaz' gene was a mix of 2 genes" fits this: Topaz = Roxi + Seymour = CEL + ALT. That's why Topaz threw compound heterozygotes, and why the line is probably lost.

## What a DBE cat looks like

**Eyes** (from the *Russian Topaz (Altai) eye colours* diagram and the prose):
- **Blue-eyed:** both eyes blue.
- **Odd-eyed:** blue + green, or blue + yellow.
- **Particoloured:** sectoral blue (see [dichroic eyes](06-eye-colour-and-shape.md#sectoral-heterochromia--dichroic-eyes)).
- **"Black-eyed":** an extremely dark blue/green iris, slightly paler near the rims, with the pupil visible only by reflected light.
- Blue and "black" DBE eyes **reflect red in camera flash**, and the blue **persists into adulthood**. It doesn't fade the way kitten blue does.

**White markings** (from the *Russian Topaz — white markings* diagram and the prose):
- There's always at least a tiny white marking. The **tail tip** is most common, then a white **locket**, white **back paws**, or a white **facial smudge**.
- Typical heterozygote: white facial markings, minimal white on the body, white back feet, white tail tip.
- The facial white progresses from a thin vertical blaze, to a wide wedge, to an inverted-V muzzle.
- Extreme white "pushes" colour to the **rear** of the body, while the white tail tip and white back feet stay. The diagram names two of these: the **Panda pattern** and the **Chipmunk pattern**.
- The size of the marking ranges from a few hairs to a large white patch (homozygotes).
- Russian Topaz lines also sometimes show **black-roan** fur.
- Messybeast's description of the "Russian blue-eye gene": the cats are selected for eye colour rather than coat pattern, and the gene "can also cause white markings, but the white pattern is different from that in ordinary bicolours."

**Penetrance and expressivity:**
- **Latents** carry DBE, have non-blue eyes, and can still produce blue-eyed kittens.
- **Fyodor rarely sired blue-eyed kittens unless his mates had the white spotting gene.** DBE expression looks enhanced by Ws.
- Breeder rule of thumb: odd-eyed (one blue, one green-yellow) kittens are heterozygotes and fine to breed from; two-blue-eyed kittens may be homozygotes and shouldn't be bred. *This is a heuristic. Heterozygotes can have two blue eyes too.*

## Mechanism

- Blue eyes and white spots can come from a disturbance at any of four steps in pigment-cell development:
  1. development and migration of **melanoblasts** from the neural crest
  2. differentiation into **melanocytes**
  3. melanocyte **survival**
  4. **melanosome/melanin** synthesis
- Genes involved in melanoblast migration, survival and proliferation:
  - transcription factors **PAX3**, **SOX10**, **MITF**
  - the **WNT** pathway
  - **EDNRB** and its ligand **EDN3**
  - **KIT** and **KITLG**
- **Human analogue: Waardenburg syndrome.** Variants in PAX3, SOX10, MITF, EDN3, EDNRB and KITLG cause this group of auditory-pigmentary disorders: blue eyes or heterochromia, sensorineural deafness, a white forelock, and dystopia canthorum (laterally displaced inner eye corners). There are four recognised types.
- **In cats, the KIT white locus** has two retroviral insertions: **wS** (spotting), with no deleterious phenotype described, and **W** (dominant white), associated with deafness. Both can produce heterochromia or blue eyes.
- **Why white and deafness travel together:** melanin is involved in the development of the inner ear, where sound vibrations are detected. Missing melanocytes affect the ear as well as the eye.
  - An experimental colony of white cats had **67% deaf** (heritability 0.55) and **29% partial hearing** (heritability 0.75).
  - That pattern suggests a pleiotropic major gene plus polygenes.
  - **BAER** testing (brainstem auditory evoked response) is used by many European breeders.

## Ojos Azules (it isn't DBE)

- **Where and when:** a dominant phenotype found in the USA in the 1980s, combining heterochromia or two blue eyes with minimal white. It was reported in a TICA journal in 1984, and TICA accepted the breed for registration in 1991. The eye-colour chart describes it as "cornflower blue eyes with non-white fur".
- **The defect:** controlled breeding by **Solveig Pflueger MD** and others produced **white kittens with cranial deformities** from two blue-eyed parents. These were presumed to be homozygotes. Waardenburg genes, PAX3 included, were the candidates.
- **The decline:** incomplete penetrance was confirmed when blue-eyed kittens were born to two non-blue-eyed parents. Together with the deformities, that ended the breed. **It is considered extinct.**
- **Not ancestral:** the breed never left America, and the modern DBE mutations don't share its side-effects. Some Altai and Topaz cats were registered *as* Ojos Azules only because it was the one DBE breed TICA recognised.

## Altai (Altay) history

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Early 1980s | Olga Volynska notices blue-eyed cats at a factory. Her report is largely ignored, because "everyone knew" blue eyes without white couldn't exist. A couple of years later (1984) the Ojos Azules is reported. |
| 1995–96 | Vera Sokolova of **Ust-Kamenogorsk, Kazakhstan** owns **Fyodor** (Theodore): black, blue eyes, white tail tip. Reported by the cat magazine *Friend* (issue 8/9, Aug–Sep 2004). |
| 1997 | **Lyubov Borisovna Zikeeva** (Darlin/Darlene cattery) notices blue-eyed street cats. The first Ust-Kamenogorsk Altai she records is a domestic semi-longhair with white marks, a blue right eye and a yellow-green left eye. After talking to Fyodor's owner, she starts the breed. |
| — | Fyodor mates many unrelated females but rarely sires blue-eyed kittens unless the queen carries white spotting. Eventually **Ariel** is born, the progenitor of all living Altai. |
| — | Blue × blue matings show **homozygotes are viable** (unlike Ojos Azules), with more white, and most often deaf. A non-lethal gene means the breed is viable. |
| G3 | Line-breeding fixes the type. A **green-eyed European Shorthair** outcross is needed to avoid inbreeding depression. |
| Now | Developed under the **World Cat Federation**. English speakers often mislabel Altai and the Ukrainian **Topaz** as "Russian Ojos Azules". Topaz combined two blue-eyed lines, differs in white-marking pattern, and sometimes shows black-roan fur. |

**Breeding guidance (Altai):** breed blue- or odd-eyed Altai to **non-blue-eyed Altai, preferably green-eyed**. That gives a mix of blue-eyed, odd-eyed and green-eyed kittens. Avoid blue × blue: homozygotes carry the same deafness risk as blue-eyed whites and blue-eyed high-white bicolours.

## Other blue-eye lines and oddities

- **Unformalised strain names:** "Barnaul DBE", "Australian DBE", "unknown DBE", named by region or founder to tell strains apart. Formal names arrive as each mutation is identified.
- **Windellama, NSW, Australia:** A. M. Schnieder found a grey bicolour (tuxedo) cat on a country road with intense **sapphire** blue eyes. Ojos Azules was never imported to Australia, so this is a spontaneous mutation in the feral population. Whether it matches the American mutation is unknown.
- **Other spontaneous blue-eye mutations** (independent of coat colour) turn up from time to time. Most are **pale** blue rather than intense, and none has been deliberately bred. The eye chart's "**Ice Blue** — occasional mutation" is probably one of these.
- **David Karamatic's line (Australia):** in this high-white line, blue eyes look **recessive** and don't track the amount of facial white.
  - The founder was a mostly white colony male with tabby markings and a tabby cap. His mother and sister had the same pattern but were odd-eyed.
  - Bred to two bicolour females: many high-white bicolours, but **no** blue or odd eyes.
  - Bred to one of his daughters: a **blue-eyed calico** and an **odd-eyed van** male.
  - So facial white isn't the only factor in blue-eye inheritance in bicolours. Other genes may be at work.

## Naming

- "**Altai DBE**" was the original umbrella term. It is now split by mutation:
  - **DBE^CEL [DBE-1]** — Roxi
  - **DBE^RE [DBE-2]** — Rociri Elvis
  - **DBE^ALT [DBE-3]** — Fyodor/Seymour
- Western fancies use **DBE + suffix**. China uses **DBE-1, DBE-2…** because the Western breed names don't translate meaningfully.
- Practical rule: always say *which* DBE you mean, because the alleles must not be crossed.

## Modelling notes (for the calculator)

*These are my design suggestions, not source content.*

- **New autosomal locus `DBE` (PAX3)**, with alleles `+` (wild), `ALT`, `CEL`, `RE`, and perhaps `?` for "unknown lineage" so users can model an untyped DBE cat. Dominance: any DBE allele over `+`.
- **Phenotype outputs:**
  - **Eyes:** a qualitative distribution (blue, odd, particoloured, latent/non-blue), not a single colour. The sources give **no penetrance figures**, so don't invent percentages; say "may be latent".
  - **White:** "minimal DBE markings" (tail tip, back feet, facial blaze). This is a separate layer from Ws grade, which means the white descriptor has to become additive: KIT grade + DBE markings.
- **Warnings, by genotype:**

  | Genotype | Warning |
  |---|---|
  | `RE/+` | Deafness association |
  | `RE/RE` | Presumed lethal |
  | `CEL/CEL` | White; the only recorded kitten had a cleft palate and died at birth |
  | `ALT/ALT` | High white or all white; deafness risk |
  | `CEL/ALT` | White; high deafness risk |
  | `ALT` or `RE` heterozygote | "Hearing not BAER-characterised" note |

- **Litter mode:** DBE × DBE gives ¼ homozygous. The existing pairing-level warning infrastructure already handles the Wd/Wd ¼ case the same way. Add the specific "Roxi-origin × Seymour-origin" warning for CEL × ALT pairings.
- **Interactions:**
  - **Ws** enhances expression (the Fyodor anecdote). Record it as a note, not a number.
  - On **cs/cs** cats DBE is invisible in eye colour, since they're blue already: note "masked by colourpoint blue".
  - On **Wd** whites it's moot.
- **Portrait:** red flash-shine and the "black" iris are optional details. Odd eyes need no new plumbing, because `catSVG` already fills each iris separately (`eyeL`/`eyeR`).
- **Prerequisite:** a real eye-colour model ([06](06-eye-colour-and-shape.md#modelling-notes)), so DBE has something to override.

## Source quirks & open questions

- **Penetrance per allele:** no numbers in the notes. They're needed before the litter odds can say anything about eye colour.
- **Igor line:** which allele? It isn't assigned in the collated text.
- **Maine Coon wording:** messybeast says "There is also DBECEL [DBE-1] (in one line of Maine Coons)". The paper's *single* Maine Coon lineage is **DBE^RE** (Rociri Elvis). DBE^CEL is *also* in a Maine Coon line, but a Topaz-founded one. Both facts are true; that sentence probably meant RE.
- **The "particular repartition" quote:** "Bicolour cats have a particular repartition of white and coloured areas in the fur, distinct from the well-known white spotting phenotype caused by the wS allele." The surrounding context in the paper wasn't collated, so it's unclear which bicolour it refers to (possibly the Ragdoll bicolour).

- **Hearing in ALT heterozygotes** is "assumed normal" with no BAER data.
- **Karamatic's recessive-looking line:** is it a Ws modifier, an undiscovered locus, or another DBE-like gene?
