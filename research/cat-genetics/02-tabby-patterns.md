# Tabby patterns & breed pattern terms

> **Sources:** messybeast.com colour & pattern guide (Sarah Hartwell), user screenshots — Tabby Patterns; Breed-Specific Tabby/Spotted Terms.  
> **Calculator status:** ✅/🟡 The domestic patterns are modelled (Mc, Sp, Ta, plus O/o for torbies): mackerel, classic, spotted, broken, ticked and torbie. ❌ Marble, clouded leopard, rosettes, braided/candle-flame and Sokoke are not; they are wild-derived or breed-selected.

## At a glance

- **15 pattern rows and 22 breed-specific rows.** The source's header box says the usual patterns are Classic (Blotched), Mackerel (Striped), Spotted, Ticked Tabby (with some striping) and Ticked/Agouti (no striping), plus modified versions seen in certain breeds.
- **The usual patterns already exist in the engine.** `Mc` gives mackerel or classic, `Sp` gives spotted or broken, `Ta` gives ticked, and `O/o` gives torbies (patched tabbies). Only the naming layer differs ("Oyster", "Spotted", "Unpatterned").
- **Six patterns sit outside the Mendelian model:** Marble, Clouded Leopard, Rosette, Oceloid, Braided/Candle Flame and Sokoke. They come from wild-cat ancestry (Bengal, Highland Lynx, Safari, Australian Mist) or from breed selection (Toyger, Sokoke). Most are better shown as a descriptive note than as a genotype.
- **The breed-specific table is mostly colour names on a spotted tabby.** Egyptian Mau, Ocicat, Bengal, Australian Mist, California Spangled and American Lynx each reuse everyday colours under their own words (Bronze, Sienna, Sorrel, Peach, Tawny). That is a registry "dialect" layer, not new genetics.
- **10 of the 22 breed rows have no description,** just a breed in brackets. Their meaning is inferred from neighbouring rows and flagged with "?".

The source's header box: the usual tabby patterns are Classic (Blotched) Tabby, Mackerel (Striped) Tabby, Spotted Tabby, Ticked Tabby (with some striping) and Ticked/Agouti (with no striping). There are also several modified versions of these seen in certain breeds.

## Modelling notes

- **Already modelled:** `Mc` (Taqpep) mackerel/classic, `Sp` (spotted/broken, needs at least one Mc) and `Ta` (DKK4, epistatic over Sp). All of these are in the engine and name correctly. Ta/ta adds a residual-barring note, which is the closest match to the source's "ticked tabby with some striping".
- **Naming-only (a registry/breed "dialect" layer could cover them):** Oyster (= classic), bare "Spotted" and "Leopard" (Bengal), and "Unpatterned Tabby" for Ta/Ta. Also Egyptian Mau Bronze/Smoke/Pewter, Ocicat Tawny/Sienna/Golden/Cinnamon-Golden, Bengal Sorrel/Snow/Mink, and Australian Mist Peach. The Ocicat and Bengal rows all resolve to colours the engine can already make (black, cinnamon, fawn, chocolate on a spotted tabby).
- **Need new loci or alleles (or a descriptive flag):** Marble and Clouded Leopard (wild-derived marbling), rosettes (Bengal, Safari), Sokoke's agouti-in-solid-areas look, and braided/candle-flame (Toyger). Own suggestion: treat these as a "hybrid / selected pattern" note on the result rather than a genotype, until a locus is characterised.
- **Show polygenic traits as a range or note, not a Mendelian result (inference):** spot roundness and alignment, how clean the legs and tail are in "unpatterned" ticked cats, and the richness of ground colour ("bright" vs "dark" cinnamon, "ruddy" vs "buff"). The calculator already calls `Sp` "uncloned, probably polygenic".
- **Pattern word is dropped for shaded and golden coats:** once `Wb` and agouti give a shaded or golden coat, the name omits the tabby/spotted/ticked word. A "Golden Leopard" or "Snow Leopard" therefore cannot express its spotted base in the name today.
- **Torbie only on non-shaded agouti:** the engine says "Torbie" when A/- and not shaded; otherwise it says "Tortoiseshell". Compare the source's "Silver Patched Tabby" and "Golden Patched Tabby" in [03](03-silver-golden-shading.md).
- **Snow Bengals map onto the C locus** (point, mink, sepia, via `cs`/`cb`) with a spotted pattern under the points; the engine already prints a note for this. A "Snow" naming alias would be enough, but Snow Marble needs the marble pattern.
- **Eye colour:** none of these rows depends on eye colour in the source. See [06](06-eye-colour-and-shape.md) for how eyes are handled.

## Source quirks & open questions

- **"Leopard" appears twice** with different meanings: in Tabby Patterns (a hybrid-cat spotted pattern, Bengal and Highland Lynx) and in the breed table (Bengal: black spots/rosettes on orange/tawny). Likewise **"Gold"** (Australian Mist and California Spangled) and **"Snow Leopard"** (American Lynx and California Spangled) are each two rows, and the California Spangled one is the only one with a description.
- **10 breed rows have no description** (Bronze, Charcoal, Ebony Leopard, both Golds, Golden Leopard, Pewter Spotted, Smoke Spotted, Snow Leopard (American Lynx) and Tawny). Their genotypes above are guesses.
- **"Bronze" means three things:** Egyptian Mau Bronze Spotted = chocolate spotted tabby, California Spangled Bronze (blank), and Ocicat Cinnamon-Golden (Bronze). The Mau claim is worth verifying: my recollection (not from the source) is that Mau "bronze" is the black-based brown spotted tabby, not chocolate.
- **Two Ocicat names for cinnamon spotted:** Golden Spotted (bright Cinnamon on Ivory) and Cinnamon-Golden (Bronze) Spotted (dark Cinnamon on Gold/Honey/Ivory). The difference looks like shade depth; "Golden" here is not necessarily the Wb golden series.
- **Sorrel is inconsistent:** the Bengal row says "chestnut-brown on orange/tawny", while the source's ticked table (not covered here) gives Sorrel (Abyssinian) as cinnamon, and "Chestnut" means chocolate elsewhere in the source.
- **Bengal "Mink"** is described only by its spots and ground colour ("black spots/rosettes on mahogany"), not as a point type, even though "mink" elsewhere in the source means the cb/cs Tonkinese colour.
- **"Highland Lynx" vs "American Lynx":** the pattern table cites Highland Lynx, the breed table American Lynx. It is unclear whether they are the same breed.
- **Snow Leopard (California Spangled), "the 'dilution phase'":** ambiguous between genetic dilution (d/d) and a pale point-type phase.
- **Spotted Tabby's last sentence** ("possibly a gene for spotted pattern … exists") is hedged in the source; the calculator's `Sp` locus already assumes one, flagged as uncloned and probably polygenic.
- **Rosette** is "confusingly called tricolour" in Bengal and Safari, which collides with calico/tricolour in [04](04-tortie-white-van.md).
- **Lead (not in the source; verify):** in **Bengals**, "charcoal" is attributed to the Asian leopard cat's own agouti allele, **APb**, usually as APb/a (Gershony et al. 2014, *Animal Genetics*). If that holds, it's a real, DNA-testable extra allele for the calculator's `A` locus, and the first wild-derived pattern gene that could be modelled in a Mendelian way. The California Spangled "Charcoal" row above may or may not be the same thing.
- **Ticked Tabby** is described as "intermediate between Agouti and Tabby", which is the source's way of separating it from Ticked/Agouti (no striping). That distinction is why Ta/ta vs Ta/Ta is labelled "?" above.
