# Conformation: body, head, ears & coat vocabulary

> **Sources:** messybeast.com *Plain English Guide to Conformation* and its glossary (Sarah Hartwell) · user diagram *Body & face types*, with its ear-set, modified-type and abnormality panels.
> **Calculator status:** 🟡 portrait only. The portrait switches to a foreign (Oriental) body, low-set ears and upturned eyes **only for hairless and rex textures** (the `FOREIGN` texture list in `CatArt`). There's no body, head or ear-set input. Nothing here is modelled genetically. Conformation is overwhelmingly **polygenic plus selection**, and the few single-gene shape traits in the diagram (short legs, radial hypoplasia) aren't in the engine.
> **Related:** [ears, skull & brain](09-ears-skull-and-brain.md) · [eye shape](06-eye-colour-and-shape.md#eye-shapes) · [health](11-health-and-welfare.md) · [coverage map](00-coverage-map.md)

## At a glance

- **Body type is a spectrum:** Foreign (Oriental) → Semi-foreign → Intermediate/Medium → Semi-cobby → Cobby. That's "long & lean shape" → "short & rounded shape".
- **Face type has seven steps:** Extreme foreign · Less extreme foreign · Semi-foreign · Intermediate · Semi-cobby · Cobby · Ultra-type cobby.
- **The labels are relative and registry-specific.** One breed can be "cobby" in Europe and "semi-cobby" in the USA, and the European Burmese is foreign where the American one is semi-cobby.
- **For the calculator:** this is a **type layer** for the portrait and a future breed-preset system, not genetics.

## Head & face shapes

| Shape | Description |
|---|---|
| **Wedge** | The face of modern foreign breeds. From the top or front, straight lines run from the outer ear bases down the sides of the muzzle, with no break at the whiskers. Flattened skull; the straight nose continues the forehead; a long narrow triangle with large, wide-set ears. It has become more exaggerated in recent decades. In profile, a mid-face bump gives a **Roman nose**; in the extreme ("banana-shaped") form it's a fault. Extreme wedges are called "bat-eared" or "spaghetti-nosed" |
| **Modified wedge** | Less extreme. The ears flare less, and the nose is less long and narrow. Still triangular. In profile the nose is straight or slightly indented, with no Roman nose. Slightly rounded, without flat planes |
| **Round** | Persian and Exotic. Short nose, broad skull, rounded cheeks, an indentation where muzzle meets face, small wide-set ears. In ultra-types the nose itself may look indented and the brow protruding |
| **Medium / intermediate** | The "normal" cat face. Moderately rounded but not massive, medium ears, a nose stop, whisker pads |

**General ear variation** (from the Toyger article, [09](09-ears-skull-and-brain.md)):
- Cats generally have prick (upright) ears, triangular in shape.
- **Size and set** range from small Persian ears to the "bat-like" flared ears of some Siamese. Some breeds need a more widely flared base than others.
- **Placement:** some breeds have ears close together, high on the head; others are wide-set with a broad forehead between.
- **Tips** can be rounded, pointed, tufted or fringed, depending on breed.

## Registry variation

- Some breeds that are "cobby" in Europe count as "semi-cobby" in the USA. European registries don't allow the same extremes of massive build, and all the terms are relative.
- **Burmese** looks distinctly foreign in Europe and tends to semi-cobby, with a domed head, in the USA.
- **European Tonkinese** looks more foreign than American Tonkinese, because its European Burmese parent is more foreign.
- Not every registry permits ultra-types, so not every term applies in every region.

## Glossary, grouped

Colour and pattern terms from the same glossary live in the colour files: FROSTED, REVERSED TICKING, GROUND COLOUR and UNDERCOLOR in [03](03-silver-golden-shading.md); GLOVES, LACES, LOCKET, GROIN SPOT and MITTED in [04](04-tortie-white-van.md); MASK, HOOD and POINTS in [05](05-colourpoint-mink-sepia-mocha.md).

### Show & registration
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **AOC** (Any Other Colour) | A colour or pattern not normally accepted in a breed, or one with no class of its own at a show. Some registries subdivide breeds into colour classes, treating each colour like a breed |
| **AOV** (Any Other Variety) | Cats from registered parents that can't enter championship classes because they don't meet the official requirements for coat colour, coat length or other traits |
| **Stacked** | Standing with weight evenly on all four feet |
| **Traditional / old-style / old-fashioned type** | The form of a now ultra-typed breed (e.g. Persian, Siamese) before "refinement" |
| **Apple-headed** | The rounded head of older-type foreign breeds, like the Siamese of the early 1900s. The name comes from a series of "applehead" dolls once popular in the USA |
| **Doll-faced** | The older Persian/Exotic face, like the Persian/Angora type of the early 1900s |
| **Ultra-type / extreme-type** | Breed traits taken to exaggerated extremes. If the standard asks for a short muzzle, the ultra-type has almost no muzzle. "Open-ended breed standards are largely to blame" |

### Head, face & jaw
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Break** | Visible indentation of the nose below or between the eyes, so the face looks pushed in |
| **Stop** | A change in direction: the short incline between the forepart of the skull and the muzzle. The concave curve in the nose at or just below eye level, slight to pronounced |
| **Muzzle break / whisker break** | The change of direction between the muzzle and the cheekbones |
| **Roman nose** | Arched (convex) nose. Some extreme Siamese look convex side-on |
| **Banana-nosed** | An undesirably exaggerated Roman nose (extreme Orientals) |
| **Spaghetti-nosed** | An undesirably long, narrow muzzle (Orientals) |
| **Snub** | Short muzzle, turned upwards |
| **Receding nose** | The nose sits further back than the chin |
| **Peke-, piggy- or pug-faced** | The ultra-typed Persian/Exotic face. Very domed head, compressed muzzle, snub nose almost flat to the face; the top of the nose leather is level with or above the lower eyelid. Can cause a protruding lower jaw and absent or compressed tear ducts |
| **Overshot jaw** | Top jaw longer than bottom |
| **Undershot** | Bottom jaw longer than top. A fault in some Persians: a short upper jaw and snub muzzle with a longer lower jaw gives a bulldog or boxer look |
| **Domed** | Hemispherical or rounded |
| **Concave / convex** | Curve like the inside / outside arc of a circle |
| **Mutton chops** | Hair below the ears and on the cheeks grows downward, like a man's mutton-chop whiskers or a lynx's ruff |

### Body, legs & tail
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Compact** | Closely and neatly packed together; the opposite of rangy |
| **Rangy** | Fairly long torso, with legs long enough to match; the opposite of compact |
| **Long and substantial** | A long-bodied cat that isn't foreign (tubular) in type |
| **Depth of flank** | Great in Manx and Cymric, minimal in American Cornish Rex |
| **Tucked up / tummy tuck** | See the table above |
| **Cowhocked** | Back legs not parallel, hocks very close together ("a bit like knock-kneed") |
| **Splayed** | Spreading outward, broad and flat |
| **Brush (1)** | Tail like a fox's brush |
| **Pom pom tail** | The effect of the knotted/fused vertebrae and fur of the Japanese Bobtail |

## Modelling notes

*Design suggestions plus clearly labelled leads; nothing here is source content.*

- **Portrait type layer:** generalise `geo.foreign` (currently a boolean set by coat texture) into a `type` with **body** (5 steps), **face** (7) and **ear set** (4), plus an eye shape ([06](06-eye-colour-and-shape.md#eye-shapes)). These are phenotype inputs, not loci.
- **Breed presets** bridge everything. A breed would fix the type, the allowed colours, the eye-colour standard and the **naming dialect** ([01](01-colour-names.md)). That's how "Seal Mink" turns into "Natural Mink" or "Natural" for a Tonkinese.
- **Single-gene shape traits the engine could gain.** *These are leads from general cat genetics, not from these notes; verify before modelling:*
  - Scottish **Fold** (dominant, with osteochondrodysplasia)
  - **Manx** tailless (dominant, homozygous lethal)
  - **Japanese Bobtail** (recessive)
  - **Munchkin** short legs (dominant)
  - **Polydactyly**
  - **LaPerm** (dominant rex)
  - **Toyger rounded ears** (GDF7) is in the notes → [09](09-ears-skull-and-brain.md)
- **Welfare framing:** the diagram files "tucked up (2)" and "radial hypoplasia" under *Abnormalities*. If the calculator ever adds shape traits, it should carry warnings the way `Hp/Hp` and `Wd/Wd` do ([11](11-health-and-welfare.md)).

## Source quirks & open questions

- **Primordial flap** appears in the diagram but not in the glossary; the gloss here is mine.
- **Short legs** and **radial hypoplasia** appear in the diagram with no genetics attached. Is radial hypoplasia separate from short-legged Munchkin-type dwarfism? The "squitten" label suggests it is.
- **Russian and Abyssinian** are foreign in some registries and semi-foreign in others. Any preset needs a registry choice.
